# Phase 24 — Research checkpoint / decision (2026-10-24)

**Status:** PHASE 24 EXPERIMENT COMPLETE; frozen analyses and independent full reranking verifier PASSED. **All three precommitted across-frame practical-impact hypotheses NOT SUPPORTED.** This does NOT deny exact reprints or scoring bias in certain conditions. No new retriever, raw-OCR benchmark, human relevance assessment, or paper is claimed.

## Research uncertainty addressed

Phase23 intentionally selected 50% exact repeated anchors, which established a strong scoring discrepancy on a reprint-enriched evaluation. It did NOT measure the frequency of this problem in ordinary content. Phase24 draws new source-derived 13-token anchors **without conditioning on repeats** and tests two defensible but different sampling units.

The corpus contains 3,608 raw pages across 718 issues (517 IMVO, 201 ISIG). Each issue contributes two independent SHA256-seeded 13-token anchors from 50-350-token raw paragraphs, yielding 1,436 queries. Frame A samples eligible page, paragraph, then eligible anchor uniformly in sequence (paragraph-oriented). Frame B samples a uniformly random eligible 13-token starting position across all eligible issue paragraphs (text-position-weighted). A nonverbatim reordered/sorted six-keyword proxy is constructed, preserving no contiguous anchor three-word span. Both sources are macro-averaged equally; issue-grouped paired 10,000-replicate bootstrap provides descriptive intervals. Every exact multi-page gold label is generated by a full corpus raw paragraph-contained 13-word match, independent of model rankings. The full 3,608-page index remains unchanged.

### Main results (source-equal macro)

| Endpoint | Frame A: paragraph-oriented | Frame B: text-position-weighted |
|---|---:|---:|
| Exact cross-issue duplication | **17.12%**, 95% CI [14.56,19.66] | **3.12%**, [1.89,4.49] |
| BM25 origin-page Top-1 | 81.79% | 91.27% |
| BM25 exact-evidence Top-1 | 92.40% | 93.12% |
| BM25 evidence minus origin | **+10.61 pp**, [8.50,12.88] | **+1.85 pp**, [0.97,2.89] |
| BM25+fuzzy origin-page Top-1 | 67.09% | 80.82% |
| BM25+fuzzy exact-evidence Top-1 | 76.87% | 82.58% |
| Fuzzy-minus-BM25, origin | −14.70 pp | −10.45 pp |
| Fuzzy-minus-BM25, evidence | −15.53 pp | −10.54 pp |
| Evaluation-induced comparison change | **−0.83 pp** [−1.75,0.00] | **−0.10 pp** [−0.48,+0.19] |

**Source heterogeneity:** exact cross-issue duplication in frame A = IMVO 27.27%, ISIG 6.97%; in frame B = IMVO 4.26%, ISIG 1.99%. Differences in content, page/paragraph structure and exposure are possible. No causes have been isolated, and these rates are NOT an estimate of natural human queries or of either language's inherent properties.

### Precommitted hypothesis decisions

- **H24a NOT SUPPORTED across frames.** Frame A meets its >=5% repeat-rate / lower-CI >2% practical gate, but B does not (3.12%, lower 1.89%). This is a meaningful sampling sensitivity.
- **H24b NOT SUPPORTED across frames.** Frame A exceeds the +5-point origin-label undercount threshold (+10.61), but B falls below (+1.85). Positive B uncertainty nevertheless establishes that some exact-repeat scoring differences persist under text-position weighting.
- **H24c NOT SUPPORTED in either frame.** Both fuzzy-vs-BM25 evaluation interaction magnitudes fall far below 5 points, with source-stratified intervals not excluding zero. Phase23's baseline-ranking sensitivity does not transfer at a practically material magnitude to these unconditioned samples.

These gates were frozen BEFORE sampling anchors or evaluating methods; they are practical falsification rules, not independent discovery p-values.

### Pre-specified previously unused issue sensitivity

Only **31 IMVO** and **zero ISIG** issues were never query origins in Phases18–23. In the 31 untouched Imvo issues, frame A repeats = 8/31 (25.8%); BM25 origin/evidence Top-1 = 61.3/80.6%. In frame B repeats = 1/31 (3.2%); BM25 origin/evidence Top-1 = 80.6/83.9%. This is directionally compatible with the source-wide sampling contrast but small and **not** a balanced independent two-newspaper test.

### Post-result mechanistic diagnostic — EXPLORATORY, NOT CONFIRMATORY

In frame A, **108/155** repeat anchors originated in 50–74-token paragraphs; frame A repeat anchors have median paragraph length 65 tokens vs 88 for nonrepeats. In frame B, repeat anchors' median paragraph length was 74 vs 130.5 for nonrepeats. Source-macro paired frame A minus B repeat prevalence is +14.0 pp (descriptive 95% interval [+11.2,+16.8]). These observations are consistent with short paragraphs receiving disproportionate weight under the paragraph sampling scheme, but **genre is unannotated**; do not automatically call all repeated text advertisements, linguistic reuse, or equivalent historical claims.

## Validity and interpretation

- Positive narrow result: *The unit of evidence sampling is an important benchmark-design variable.* The estimated magnitude of exact-reprint label ambiguity changes markedly when every paragraph versus every eligible text position receives weight. Results cannot be generalized to actual query prevalence or semantic relevance.
- Phase23 still valid for its intentionally repetition-enriched sample. It should not be generalized as a 44.6-point estimate for a naturally sampled archive or used to claim fuzzy rankings generically reverse.
- Single-origin scoring is correct for queries asking about **a specific issue/page**. Multi-gold exact evidence can be correct for **verbatim content retrieval**; neither label set alone covers context-sensitive historical semantics.
- New query origins are not all untouched: no eligible Isigidimi issues remain issue-unseen across previous experiments. Although Phase24 samples NEW anchor strings by a deterministic method, the archive has already guided multiple earlier hypotheses. The previously untouched Imvo subset is reported separately.
- Both queries and gold anchors are **source-derived**, not independent historical research questions; no human judgments, no isiXhosa specialist annotation, no verified machine OCR.
- A and B sample **eligible** 13-word locations in 50–350-token paragraphs using a keyword-constructible filter; they do not sample all words/pages or actual user searches. CI describes the fixed stratified issue-sampling protocol, not a wider population of archival use.
- `raw` text is a human corrected/transcribed archive representation; historical OCR error cannot be quantified using this corpus alone. Exact 13-word agreement is not an exhaustive semantic relevance set.
- The method is unchanged word BM25/fuzzy; no novel retrieval algorithm developed.

## Integrity/reproducibility

`PRECOMMIT.md` SHA256 was recorded before anchor selection. `sample_frozen.jsonl` was separately hashed before duplicate labels were assembled; `queries_gold_frozen.jsonl` was hashed before any new model ranking. All 718 issues were eligible. All 1,436 labels were reconstructed from an independent single-pass raw archive scan. `verify_phase24.py` confirms hashes, paragraph boundaries, source pages, exact full-archive gold, all query transformations, every saved full-corpus BM25 score (max abs difference **0.0**), all 1,436 pairs of Top-20 rankings, source macro outcomes, bootstrap CIs and precommitted gates. **PASS.** Scripts and frozen query/score outputs retained; source corpus not re-distributed.

## Decision and stop condition

**Reject the broad claim that reprints systematically change benchmark method selection under unconditioned evidence sampling.** Retain the experimentally supported narrower finding that repeated content can materially distort exact-page evaluation in short/paragraph-weighted or reprint-enriched subsets.

**Stop further source-derived or synthetic benchmark-variant sweeps** without new data, because all Isigidimi query-origin issues have now been exposed and keyword queries are not genuine information needs. The next research gate is external data acquisition: (1) independently written historical research questions with blind human/expert judgments of answer-bearing passages AND page provenance; (2) verifiably raw scan-aligned machine-OCR exports with provenance/rights; (3) then competitive lexical/dense benchmark comparison on an evaluation set that was not used for method design. If those data cannot be obtained, document that limitation; do not manufacture novelty or another synthetic success.

**Scientific contribution now:** a benchmark validity/sampling-choice finding, not an original retrieval algorithm or published-level external validation. No paper/abstract drafted.
