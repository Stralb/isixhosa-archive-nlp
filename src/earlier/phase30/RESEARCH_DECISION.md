# Phase 30 - Mechanism and limits of source-specific BPE segmentation
24 October 2026 | Scientific checkpoint | *Not a cultural-evolution or sentiment paper*

## Primary research question
If **the exact same historical surface word** appears in both newspapers, does its frequency in source-specific tokenizer training explain which model segments it more compactly? Does this account for the source-specific gap beyond arbitrary training-file partitioning?

## Confirmed computational facts (prospective follow-up; 1888 already analyzed in prior phases)
- Six frozen BPE-1000 models trained on 1885/1887 source-specific excerpts; three paired seeds; 60,000 alphabetic training characters per newspaper and seed. Evaluation: 207 held-out-year excerpt windows from 52 Imvo and 12 Isigidimi issues, 90 words each. These are *not* newly untouched pages for the research programme; their use across Phases26-29 is disclosed.
- 1,140 exact word-form types appear in **both** newspapers' 1888 evaluation excerpts. 716, 719 and 702 of these pass the frozen pooled-training-frequency >=2 filter across the three model pairs.
- **H30A supported (type-level):** Correlating source-relative *whole-word training frequencies* with signed differences between the two BPE segmentations of the **same exact word**, the three Spearman coefficients are **0.505, 0.495 and 0.472**. All three pass length- and training-frequency-stratified permutation tests (p = 1/2001, or 0.00050); type bootstrap intervals approximately [0.456,0.552], [0.437,0.546], [0.420,0.525]. Source/editor-name masking hardly changes rho (0.506,0.492,0.472). A posthoc first-four-character string-family bootstrap also remains positive in all seeds; first-four-character group is **not** a morphology label.
- Most identical forms tie between models (515/716, 527/719, 524/702 in the three seeds). In the subset for which frequency and piece-count differences are both nonzero, the signs agree 95.5%, 95.3% and 92.1% (posthoc descriptive concordance; not the precommitted metric). This is evidence of source-frequency alignment, not proof that whole-word frequency alone causes every learned BPE merge.
- **H30B not supported in both newspaper groups:** Restricting held-out evaluation to word types shared between newspapers leaves mean specialist savings per 100 alphabetic characters **+0.362 Imvo** and **+0.806 Isigidimi**. Issue/seed bootstrap 95% intervals **[-0.331, 1.232]** and **[0.043,1.514]**, respectively. The Imvo interval crosses zero. Shared word types contribute 35.1% and 37.4% of the evaluated character mass; the remainder consists of types observed in just one paper's 1888 sample. Neither the exclusive type set nor shared type set constitutes a linguistically annotated semantic class.
- **H30C supported against precommitted source-blind word-shuffle controls:** Combined source-specific advantage S = Imvo own-tokenizer savings + Isigidimi own-tokenizer savings, observed **1.794 pieces/100 alphabetic characters**, against a mean **0.159 absolute S** for randomized source-blind, equal-budget control pairs. The precommitted paired issue/seed bootstrap for observed minus control is **[1.196, 2.809]**, above zero.
- **Later prospective sensitivity after the main result, not primary confirmation:** To address loss of word-order and line-boundary structure in the first placebo, source-blind BPE pairs were retrained after *exactly partitioning complete training lines* into 30,000 characters from each newspaper in each control model. All original training lines were preserved and split with no overlap. Their mean absolute S was only **0.051**, and the observed minus control paired interval was **[1.060,2.791]**; the frozen sensitivity gate passed. This control is a stronger rebuttal to the objection that the first null trained on random word salads.

## What this genuinely adds
Phase 29 found that differences in word length did not materially explain source-specific BPE efficiency. Phase 30 goes further by *holding exact evaluation word identity and length fixed*, and demonstrating a consistent association between **training-frequency exposure and piece-count advantage**, independently of source identification or different held-out word spellings. Both independently constructed randomized-source controls indicate that the observed aggregate specialization is not a generic consequence of splitting the same training pool in half.

**Evidence chain:** historical newspaper domain -> relative string/word frequencies in tokenizer training -> different segmentation choices on *identical* held-out surface words -> newspaper-domain compactness gap. This is not proof that whole-word frequency mediates all differences or that political meaning varies between those texts.

## Negative results and reviewer caveats
1. H30B failed for Imvo on shared words; **do not** claim shared words alone explain the complete effect in both corpora.
2. First-placebo word shuffling was a plausible confound; the full-line-preserving post-result sensitivity was added and disclosed, not retroactively characterized as independent confirmation.
3. Model training pools are *source-specific* and retain editorial/topic/genre/printing effects; learned unigram frequency differences correlate with BPE subword learning but do not uniquely cause it. Exact word identity eliminates length and surface-spelling differences *within a word*, but not surrounding historical context or sense ambiguity.
4. Many words have tied piece counts; the correlation mainly ranks a minority of diverging segmentations. Cluster independence at type level is imperfect; extra prefix-string bootstrap is only a sensitivity diagnostic, not a linguistically valid morphological clustering.
5. The 1888 evaluation texts were already consulted repeatedly, so these are prospective *conditional* follow-ups rather than new independent evidence.
6. Source divisions are historical newspapers, **not** dialect, culture, speaker identity, historical political attitude or fairness ground truth. The 1885 human-coded topic/article metadata in Schoots et al. (2023) remain inaccessible at item level via web (archive browser verification); no genre labels have been invented.
7. None of this measures valid historical sentiment, relative political agency, actual downstream historical-event extraction, verified raw OCR or external model performance.

## Decision
- Retain H30A and H30C as strong **computational** mechanism/negative-control findings; retain the failed H30B classification.
- Publication framing: a *historical-language measurement and representation study* linking previous source-composition intervention, source-specific BPE allocation, and downstream neural bigram effects. Phase 30 adds a within-identical-word controlled explanation and a stronger random-source control. Independent scholarly novelty still requires literature comparison; this alone is not a demonstrated novel algorithm or cultural discovery.
- Do not run more unmotivated surface-classification experiments. Next meaningful gate is external or independently validated human-coded article-level 1885 metadata; alternatively a properly independent domain/year external validation and a stronger language-model baseline if publication ambition exceeds a workshop study. No paper drafted.
